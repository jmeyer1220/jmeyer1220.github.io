Case study

Sightline lived out

This portfolio site is not just a container for work — it is a governed artifact. Symptom → force → decision → artifact. The method applied to itself.

01 — Symptom

The brand launched.
The site works.
The systems exist.
Something still feels disconnected.

The failure rarely starts where it becomes visible.

A portfolio can look finished while decisions live only in memory — typography chosen ad hoc, CTAs patched per section, taxonomy colors that run out before the third category. Visitors feel the drift even when they can't name it. Meaning leaks first.

BDR-0005

02 — Force

Where the pressure shows up here

Signal Loss — cross-cutting with governance

"Something keeps slipping between strategy and execution. I can't tell where the disconnect is."

The site encodes a diagnostic framework (three forces, self-diagnosis CTA) but design decisions weren't locked in a traceable system — every tweak risked becoming a new local rule. Signal Loss names that seam: intent decided in conversation, execution in CSS, with no artifact holding the two together. The fix isn't more polish. It's governed provenance — BDRs downstream of which code is allowed to change.

BDR-0004

03 — Decision

BDR-governed choices on this site

  • BDR-0009

    Typography migration to Instrument Sans body and Instrument Serif accent — editorial voice without flattening UI metadata.

  • BDR-0004 · BDR-0007

    Three-force IA with Work before About — evidence and taxonomy precede bio and credentials.

  • BDR-0006

    Self-diagnosis inquiry (A/B/C/D) instead of a generic contact form — diagnostic frame through conversion.

  • ADR-007

    Consolidated stylesheet — field guide and homepage share tokens in one style.css, scoped by body class.

  • BDR-0010 · BDR-0011

    Public design-system catalog and this case-study page — footer discovery, one-line provenance on public surfaces.

Debt stays visible: force-tag colors incomplete (BDR-0008), CTA roles fragmented (BDR-0002). Locked BDRs record reality — they don't pretend the site is finished.
