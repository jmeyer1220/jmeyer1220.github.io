Diagnostic Field Guide v0.2
Seam
Find where meaning starts to break down — before the big failure shows up
01 — The claim

Most organizations don't collapse overnight. The trouble usually arrives in small pieces — a message that warps, a promise that weakens, a handoff that nobody owns.

Organizations rarely break
all at once.
Meaning leaks first.

People still care. What breaks is what happens when intent passes through teams, tools, approvals, and handoffs — especially when everyone is moving fast.

02 — The unit of analysis

Don't try to fix the whole organization at once. Start with the handoffs — the places where work passes from one person, team, or system to another.

Look at the seams —
the moments where someone
has to interpret what someone else meant.

In plain terms

A seam is the handoff where meaning gets lost — like telephone or a relay race. The message can be clear at the start and wrong by the time it lands.

→ strategy to execution
→ leadership to operations
→ brand to website
→ product to onboarding
→ policy to behavior
→ diagnosis to experience
03 — Three forces to look for

When meaning leaks at a seam, one of three pressures is usually doing the work. Each force has a plain name, a feeling people recognize, and a pattern underneath.

Force What it feels like
Trust Compression Legitimacy gap "We're real — but we don't look or feel like it." The organization grew up. The brand didn't keep pace — so credibility leaks before anyone opens a spreadsheet.
Signal Loss Meaning drift "What we meant keeps changing during execution." Every handoff adds translation. Intent decided in one room becomes something else in the build — and nobody can point to where it shifted.
Complexity Drag Carrying capacity "Everything exists — but nothing moves cleanly anymore." Tools, docs, and rules piled up faster than the structure could hold. Work still happens — but only if someone remembers how the system was supposed to work.
04 — The diagnostic questions

Use these at any seam. You're not looking for blame — you're looking for where interpretation breaks down.

  • ◦
    Where does someone else get to decide what you meant? Seams fail when authority to interpret moves without a shared record.
  • ◦
    Where do people say "that's not what we meant"? That phrase is a seam marker — write down the handoff, not just the frustration.
  • ◦
    Which tool or doc is holding meaning it was never built to hold? Overloaded systems quietly become the real strategy — usually without a decision.
  • ◦
    What only works if the right person remembers how? Institutional memory is a seam — it breaks when people leave or scale.
  • ◦
    Which handoffs need the most translation? Heavy translation usually means the original intent didn't survive in a usable form.
  • ◦
    What does the system promise that the experience doesn't deliver? This is the Sightline question — did the system keep the promise it made?
05 — The reframe

Brand isn't just how you look. It's the part of the system that has to carry meaning into the real world.

Branding is not just communication.
It's the system responsible for helping
meaning survive contact with reality.
The question isn't whether your organization has intent.
The question is whether your systems can carry it intact.
